Mosque location reliability and feedback

During an enabled prayer window, a person entering their saved mosque should find the phone quiet. Someone passing the same wall should keep their sound. When the person leaves, the app should promptly release its own Do Not Disturb (DND) rule.

Measure where the process breaks, give people a way to tell us what actually happened, and let them correct the shared mosque map. Use the results to choose between fixing a boundary, Android integration, or the detection logic. Verify the change on real visits and nearby pass-bys before expanding it.

What a user can choose

On by default for development APKs and the designated Google Play beta. Opt-in for other releases. The single Help improve mosque silencing switch controls anonymous reliability metrics for each enabled prayer window and occasional optional questions. Developers and beta testers can turn it off in Settings. On other builds, collection starts only after the user turns it on. Both groups contribute the same measurements wherever regional collection is enabled. Give feedback is available to everyone.

What happens automatically in the beta
Report urgent faults as soon as the app observes them. After each enabled prayer window, send the outcome and the counts behind it, including windows with no detected arrival. The prayer label lets the team see whether, for example, a new version has a problem at Fajr while the other prayers work normally. A tester can skip any question.
When the person turns it off
Stop collecting improvement metrics and scheduling questions. Dismiss pending invitations, cancel queued metric uploads and delete unsent counters. Re-enabling starts a fresh reporting period. Previously aggregated contributions cannot be individually retrieved.
Whenever someone wants to contribute
With the switch in either position, a person can report a problem, correct a public mosque boundary or suggest a missing mosque. Show the chosen answer, pin or outline before Send. Sending authorises that one submission and leaves the switch as the person set it. Saving an edit for personal use keeps it on the phone.
Placement, wording and privacy controls

Put the switch in mosque settings. A proposed Arabic label is المساهمة في تحسين كتم الهاتف في المسجد. Where metrics and questions are active, the description reads يرسل التطبيق إحصاءات مجهولة الهوية عن أداء الكتم لكل صلاة، وقد يعرض سؤالًا اختياريًا. Where only questions are available, describe only that behaviour. The map action can say اقتراح تعديل حدود المسجد. Before Send, explain that the team receives this exact shape and may publish the approved boundary. Put payload examples and retention details behind an accessible info button.

Preview a structured technical summary separately and leave its attachment unchecked. If participation was off when a problem happened, accept the person's account with whatever they choose to describe. Request no new location permission to answer a question or manually place a pin. Keep questions out of lock-screen notifications and the active prayer window.

Apply the default-on setting to an unset preference in the dev APK or a designated Play beta build. An explicit off always wins. Preserve an explicit opt-in when switching builds. A production build drops any inherited beta default and discards unsent beta-only batches. Use a production-configured artifact when promoting a release. Play distributes the highest eligible version, so scope the behaviour to the installed build and avoid claiming to know a tester’s live enrollment. Play track delivery.

Reports include the prayer type, scheduled prayer day, app build, Android version, manufacturer and device model, with coarse durations and outcomes. Precise prayer times, visit times, mosque identifiers, coordinates, routes, step traces, free text, email addresses, IP addresses, accounts, user-assigned phone names, serial numbers, IMEIs and persistent installation identifiers stay outside the payload and retained logs. A random key deduplicates retries of one report. Keep different prayers and feedback tickets unlinkable to the same phone. Keep the existing regional exclusions and describe the fields and reporting cadence in the app, privacy policy and Play Data safety form.

Google tells beta testers that certain usage data is collected and shared. Its developer policy separately requires prominent disclosure and affirmative action for unexpected uses of personal and sensitive data. Android vitals already uses Google’s usage-and-diagnostics choice. Beta tester guidance · Play User Data policy · Android vitals coverage.

Articles 4 and 5 of Tunisia’s Organic Law 2004-63 define personal data by whether a person can be identified directly or indirectly. The reporting design requires that a recipient cannot reasonably attribute the counts to a person using the report or other available information. Keep that property when adding fields. What to measure and what it tells us

The dashboard tracks setup, monitoring, detection and Android’s response for beta participants and others who opt in. Compare the same prayer’s counts and delay ranges by build and release channel, since beta and production audiences can differ. Volunteer feedback tells us whether the phone made the right decision in the real world. Give every rate a denominator and label its source so a detector-confirmed arrival is never counted as a user-confirmed visit.

What the rollout owner sees and when

As soon as a failure is known
Queue a small fault report immediately when an eligible monitor cannot start, Android rejects an activation or release write, or the app observes its mosque rule still pending past its release deadline. Attempt delivery promptly when connected, after the sound-control action. Alert the rollout owner on the first observed overdue mosque rule. Show individual write and startup failures in the live feed, and group repeats by build, prayer and reason.
After each prayer window
Queue one summary for each eligible prayer occurrence when monitoring ends, including any bounded grace. If cleanup is still pending at the hard deadline, finalise with that outcome. Report eventual cleanup when observed. Include expected windows, on-time and missed starts, usable location, detected arrivals, fallback, Android writes, release reasons and delay bands. Count no-arrival and unknown outcomes. Send at the next connectivity opportunity without waiting for a daily or weekly batch.
On the dashboard
Start with this rollout’s latest prayer. Show report volume, the last receipt, failures by reason, and the fraction of completed windows affected. Show the manufacturer, model and Android version on each incoming fault, including the first. Filter fault reports and completed-window counts by the same manufacturer, model and Android version for the same prayer on the new and previous app builds. Show failures alongside the number of eligible attempts for that model, so a popular model does not look less reliable simply because more people use it. Keep the raw fault feed available at low volume and flag rate changes once enough completed windows arrive. Compare equivalent reporting periods while counts are still arriving. Daily and weekly views show longer trends. During the pilot, aim to display 95% of critical faults within five minutes of detection on connected phones that can run, and 95% of accepted reports within one minute of server receipt. Measure both delays. Mark late uploads using broad age bands.

A phone can report a missed start only when it next runs and reconciles its planned window. Android background limits or lost connectivity can delay any submission. Show windows still awaiting data as unknown where they can be accounted for, and surface unexpected drops in report volume separately. Test the reporting pipeline with a labelled synthetic fault before rollout so a dashboard outage is visible independently of the detector. Exclude synthetic faults from user rates. Keep delayed reports attributed to their original prayer day and build.

For example, if rule-write failures stay flat but more activations take 60 seconds or more after usable entry evidence arrives, investigate the detector. If write failures rise while detector timing holds steady, investigate the Android rule. A person who walked inside and was never detected can still report that missed arrival from the Prayer tab.

Use and setup

Among participating phones observed selecting mosque mode, measure the fraction reaching “ready” within seven days. Count common blockers separately, including missing saved area, background location, observation notifications, exact alarms and DND access. Track the share of reporting phones with mosque mode enabled. Measure week-two continued use from locally recorded eligibility and follow-up. Show the number with follow-up and the number whose status is unknown.

Observation coverage

For expected observation windows, count on-time starts, late starts, missed starts and unknowns. Record startup lateness in bands. For windows that began, count foreground-service interruptions, failed geofence registration, and periods requiring a fresh fix that received none. The healthy power-saving state parks location sampling while the geofence can wake it.

Location quality

For each window that requested a location reading, count whether the phone received a usable one and how long the first usable reading took. For inconclusive approaches, count the main problem the detector actually observed, such as stale readings, low accuracy or inconsistent positions. Record accuracy in broad metre ranges and mark unknown evidence explicitly. This shows whether to fix background delivery, sampling or the detector itself.

Arrival

For eligible windows, count no approach, approach without confirmation, provisional silence, provisional expiry and confirmed arrival. Count fallback-to-schedule separately, with its reason. For accepted arrivals, measure first usable entry evidence to silence request while silence is eligible. If someone is already inside when the silence window opens, start the delay clock at window opening. A window without an approach supplies no evidence of a missed visit.

Android applies silence

For genuine mosque-rule activation attempts, count accepted writes and write failures. Where Android supplies rule status, measure confirmations and missing callbacks separately. Record intentional Android Auto pauses, awake-check pauses, user skips and already-active manual silence as distinct context. A successful function return can also mean the operation was deliberately skipped.

Departure and restoration

For each active mosque rule due for release, record the reason, release delay, pending retry, eventual cleanup and unknown system confirmation. Reasons include detected exit, user stop, evidence expiry, scheduled end and the 15-minute grace limit. Prioritise overdue releases. Check the mosque-owned rule independently of another DND rule or the user's original silent mode.

Power and map quality

Track precise-location minutes and wake-ups per observed window, plus repeat attempts. Compare by Android major version only where volumes support it. For the catalogue, count reports by type, duplicates, cases awaiting evidence, review time, accepted fixes and fixes actually bundled in an app release. Review age and outcome together so difficult cases stay visible.

Counting rules, upload contents and delivery failures
  • Use the planner’s prayer type and prayer day. The daily labels are Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha. Jumuʿah has its own Friday window, as do Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha when enabled. Keep Isha with its scheduled prayer day across midnight. Count each planned eligible occurrence once. Join early observation, the main window and grace locally. If coverage overlaps, report coverage for each eligible prayer, count each physical Android write or release only once and label it “shared window” when its origin is ambiguous. Setup changes outside a window use an app-level category. Prayer window planner.
  • Reconcile expected windows when the scheduler or app next runs. Exclude a deliberate disable or skip from eligible failures. Locally retain only the operational state and counters needed to finalise the report. A phone that never runs again has unknown coverage.
  • Count a transition once even after a reboot, callback replay or retry. Track the terminal arrival outcome once per window, plus separate transition counters for provisional silence, confirmation and fallback. Fallback and confirmed arrival can occur in the same window. Count actual rule writes at the Android call, independently of detector decisions. Measure elapsed intervals on a monotonic clock when available.
  • Count slow confirmations by entry method (inside, edge, earlier precise reading or walking entry), provisional expiries by location-quality band, and overdue releases by trigger (outside fix, exterior return path or grace limit). Record separately how often cleanup required a retry. Compute these combinations on the phone. The per-prayer totals can then identify a failing detection path without reconstructing an individual walk.
  • For setup completion, include only selections made during an eligible measurement period with a full follow-up opportunity. For week-two use, the phone reports its local cohort and status once it returns. Publish the first-week cohort total, returning numerator and observed denominator alongside incomplete follow-up. Someone who stopped sharing or never reopened has unknown retention.
  • Allow only schema version, app build, detector version, catalogue version, release channel, prayer type and prayer day when applicable, report kind, delivery-age band, Android API level, manufacturer and device model, the reliability counters above, timing bands and a random one-report retry key. Enforce the field list at both ends. Accumulate counts in timing bands on the phone, including eligible entry evidence to silence request and detected exit to rule release. Raw event sequences stay local. Use non-overlapping delay bands of under 10 seconds, 10 to under 30, 30 to under 60, and 60 or more, plus unknown. Split counts on version change so an upgrade cannot move old failures into a new release.
  • Include the device model from the first rollout. Both immediate fault reports and completed prayer-window summaries carry manufacturer from Build.MANUFACTURER, device_model from Build.MODEL, and android_api_level from Build.VERSION.SDK_INT. Capture these when recording the outcome so an upload after an OS update retains the original context. Preserve model variants such as CPH2747. Trim surrounding whitespace, group equivalent casing for filtering, and use “Unknown” for missing values. An unrecognised model code is still useful. The dashboard can display a familiar Android version label alongside the API level.
  • Persist reports in a bounded queue and acknowledge them idempotently. Give each fault report and each window summary its own retry key. Count each distinct fault transition once locally. The fault feed provides early warnings. Final rates use the outcome counts in window summaries alone so a failure observed through both paths counts once. Retries retain the original key, day and build. Send multiple queued reports in one request after reconnection, rechecking participation and regional eligibility at dispatch. Location sampling, silencing and release always proceed independently of upload success.

Ask questions that can change a decision

The first question should work even when detection missed the whole visit. Let users open Give feedback → How did mosque silencing work? Offer “Worked as expected”, “Stayed audible inside”, “Silenced too late”, “Silenced me outside”, “Sound returned too early”, “Stayed silent after I left” and “Unsure”. Accept a single answer. Offer at most one relevant follow-up.

Missed or late entry

“Where were you when you expected silence?” Offer prayer hall, courtyard, entrance, or unsure. If the location is missing from the map, offer a separate pin submission. This distinguishes a missing site or misunderstood boundary from an arrival delay.

Silenced outside

“Were you passing, waiting outside, or in a neighbouring building?” This determines which negative case to reproduce. A private area that includes a stretch of pavement suggests a map check.

Sound restored at the wrong time

“Were you still inside when sound returned?” or “About how long after leaving did silence continue?” Offer broad duration bands and “Unsure”. Look for a grace-limit release, weak departure evidence or a failed Android cleanup.

Successful and ordinary windows

When the participation switch is on, sample a small share of eligible windows on-device, including ones with no detected arrival. On the next suitable app visit ask “Were you inside a saved mosque during this window?” Offer inside, only passed nearby, elsewhere, or unsure. If relevant, ask whether the sound behaved as expected. Show the relevant prayer in the prompt. Include its type in a submitted answer only when the preview displays it.

How to add future questions without exhausting volunteers

Start with at most one optional feedback question per 14 days, only when the app is open and outside the prayer window. Choose eligible windows by schedule on-device, independently of detected attendance. Check the participation setting before sampling and again before display. Expire invitations whose window the user is unlikely to remember. Skip dismisses that question. A settings link opens the single participation switch. User-initiated reports remain available at any time and generate no follow-up notifications.

Give each question an owner, the decision it informs, a version, eligible audience, answer choices, expiry, sample target and stop condition. If fallback rises, ask a short, time-limited question about whether the phone was actually inside. Use reviewed Arabic question templates with approved choices. Any new data category needs its own disclosure and choice. A report preview shows the selected answers and every optional field.

Analyse volunteered complaints, randomly invited answers and controlled field tests separately. Only include broad question display and response totals in automatic metrics when they qualify under the same anonymous reporting rules. Exclude question IDs or triggers that disclose a visit. Keep answer contents out of those counts. Send the chosen answers and question version only when the person taps Send. Where metrics are unavailable, nonresponses remain unknown. Even sampled responders can differ from people who never respond.

An example preview could read “You reported late silence. Monitoring started on time. The first usable location reading arrived after 30–60 seconds. Android accepted the silence request.” Include these fields only when evidence for the selected window is available on the phone. Offer “I’m unsure which window” and send the feedback on its own. The receiver can distinguish a delay in receiving location from a delay after the detector had enough evidence.

Turn a map suggestion into a catalogue fix

  1. Correct an existing boundary. From the mosque editor, offer “Suggest a boundary correction”. Show the catalogue outline and the proposal together. Offer reasons such as missing prayer hall, includes pavement or neighbour, and wrong building. A contributor can mark only the affected part. For complex outlines, preserve holes and separate buildings. Offer a marker plus reason when the editor cannot express the edit safely.
  2. Add a missing mosque. From mosque search or Give feedback, offer “Suggest a missing mosque”. Let the person tap the public building on a map, move the pin, and optionally enter its public name or draw an outline. Check nearby names and existing listings, including entries awaiting a boundary. A pin alone is useful and enters the research queue. Automatic silencing requires a reviewed eligible outline.
  3. Review and send. Show the exact selected geometry, public name if entered, reason, and whether the user marked a point, part of a boundary, or a full replacement. Explain that an approved place or outline can appear in the shared catalogue. Allow edits, cancel and send. Exclude the user's live-position dot, saved-area list, personal nicknames, visit history and device identifiers. Let users save a private edit immediately whether or not they submit.
  4. Validate and investigate. A new intake service issues a random receipt, then sends validated suggestions to a restricted reviewer queue. Check bounds, coordinate order, valid geometry and duplicates. Match a known mosque by public catalogue ID and base revision if available. A reviewer investigates new pins, compares the exact proposed shape with the current outline and imagery, and checks streets, neighbours, missing roof sections and competing listings. Overlapping or stale edits require reconciliation against the latest version.
  5. Publish and close the loop. Record accepted, duplicate, held for evidence or declined, with a short reason. Preserve the existing source and review history. Publish approved changes through the existing catalogue validator and check the bundled Android asset and eligibility flag. The receipt can distinguish “Accepted” from “Available in version X”. Allow a contributor to view status or withdraw a pending suggestion using a private receipt. A published public boundary may already be present in distributed app copies.
Map data contracts and rollout to existing phones

Send a selected public catalogue identifier and its base revision only when the app can establish them. The private area store uses device-local identifiers and saves a copy of the geometry. Add optional catalogue provenance for future imports. For older and hand-drawn areas, ask the contributor to choose the public record or submit an unlinked suggestion. The reviewer resolves uncertain matches. Keep the private area identifier off the wire.

Use a restricted proposal schema for point, annotated part, or full Polygon/MultiPolygon with valid rings. Preview WGS84 coordinates on the map and preserve GeoJSON longitude–latitude order on export. Reject invalid or out-of-scope geometry with an actionable error. Limit payload size, free text, vertices and submission frequency. Deduplicate retries by submission key. Give reviewers a side-by-side overlay, a before/after geometry diff, source revision and conflict flags. Reviewers can request one optional clarification through the receipt.

For a published fix, an unedited linked local copy can offer “Use updated boundary” with a preview. Preserve a person's custom edits and enabled state. If the phone cannot link an older private copy confidently, let the person reselect the catalogue mosque. Validate the update path on an existing installation as well as a clean one.

Prove a fix in the field

An Android callback answers whether the rule changed. A person can tell us whether they were inside. To measure doorway timing, invite pilot volunteers to mark actual entry and exit or a deliberate outside pass-by. The phone can calculate elapsed times locally and send a reviewed summary. Include windows in which the app detected nothing. The existing detailed development recorder can support a separate investigation on a test phone.

Missed arrival

Count every independently marked, eligible entry in the pilot. Report the fraction without mosque-rule activation within the agreed deadline, plus the fraction with unknown sound outcome. Measure median and 90th-percentile delay for observable activations and show the missed count beside them.

False silence

Use all labelled outside controls during eligible windows as the denominator, including sidewalk stops and visits to adjacent buildings. Count activations during those controls. Identify schedule fallback in its own breakdown so users silenced outside through fallback stay visible.

Restoration

Count releases while the volunteer was still inside, including grace expiry. Measure exit-to-release on independently marked departures. Report overdue and unknown releases, whether another rule remained active, and extended visits that exceeded the designed grace period.

Pilot scenarios, targets and release decision

Cover a small mosque against a public pavement, adjacent buildings, a multi-part site, GPS drift indoors, sparse readings with the screen locked, a person already inside when the window opens, a long stay past the scheduled end, leaving beside the same wall, Android Auto and a second DND owner. Run baseline and candidate against the same labelled cases. Reserve other sites, phones and visits for confirmation. Keep synthetic replay results distinct from live outcomes.

In eligible, independently marked field trials, aim for at least 95% of entries activate within 30 seconds, at most 1% of outside controls activate, and at least 95% of departures release within 60 seconds. Confirm these product targets after the baseline and before evaluating a candidate. Report sample sizes, uncertainty and the mix of sites and phones. Repeated walks at one mosque can dominate a small pilot. Unknown outcomes stay visible. Insufficient evidence calls for a larger pilot.

Investigate any reproducible stuck mosque rule immediately. Hold an expansion if a candidate gains entries by silencing more passers-by, increases premature releases, or materially increases precise-location time per window. Compare active monitoring minutes, wake-ups and on-device battery use under matched conditions.

Delivery and ownership

  1. Set the beta defaults. Product and Android owners document each metric’s purpose, payload, regional availability and collection basis, including what the transport and backend can associate with a sender. Agree on pilot targets and Arabic copy. Test first-run beta use without a prompt, turning participation off, production defaults, and one-off submissions in either state.
  2. Ship the smallest useful pilot. Android and backend owners deliver the live fault feed, per-prayer dashboard, labelled volunteer results, outcome report, both map submission types and a reviewer queue. Reuse the map editor and the existing local research workflow. Set up a dedicated intake endpoint that passes validated proposals to the local catalogue review workflow. Keep prayer-window summaries, support cases and public map proposals in separate stores with separate permissions. Publishing access belongs to catalogue maintainers.
  3. Measure and triage. During rollout, route critical alerts to the release owner and review each prayer as its reports arrive. Review non-urgent survey themes and the map queue weekly. Compare by build and detector version, inspect counts and unknowns, and assign each recurring problem to Android, detector or catalogue work. Record the hypothesis, baseline, owner and success measure. Add an occasional question only when it can resolve a specific uncertainty.
  4. Test, release, and follow up. Android owners reproduce defects, add matched regression cases and run the labelled field pilot. Catalogue maintainers validate and version accepted geometry. Roll out to a limited cohort first, compare against the baseline, then expand or revert the affected change. Record which app and catalogue release contains the fix and whether the original problem fell in comparable cases.

Example. Three people report being silenced on a pavement. A submitted correction shows that the published outline covers the pavement. Review the boundary, ship the verified correction and repeat the outside-stop test. If the outline is sound across several affected sites, compare provisional entries, fallback and location quality, then test a detector change on both pass-bys and genuine entries.

Assign a release owner to review an alert when activation failures or missed starts rise above the previous release’s range for the same prayer at adequate volume. A report of silence continuing beyond the app's hard deadline goes straight to Android triage even at low volume. When a fix ships, track the same denominator and repeat the outside-stop controls for any change that speeds up arrival.

Acceptance checks before enabling uploads
  • On a fresh Play beta install, verify there is no enrollment invitation. Where automatic collection is permitted, verify the actual payload contains the expected anonymous counts and timing bands. Simulate a mosque visit, a blocked region and an unknown region. Check that retries count once, separate prayer reports carry independent keys, and the endpoint and its logs retain no identifying fields. Verify that blocked regions generate no upload. Switch off while offline, reconnect and confirm pending metrics and questions were cleared. On every other build, verify zero improvement counters or uploads before opt-in and the same metric schema as beta after opt-in. Test beta-to-production promotion, an explicit opt-in surviving an upgrade, a beta default expiring, and an opt-out staying off. In either state, skipping a question sends no answer and a one-off submission sends only its previewed fields. Verify deduplication across a process restart.
  • Check a missed alarm after reboot, a planned skip, overlapping windows, no geofence wake-up, deliberate parking, fallback, provisional expiry, Android Auto, missing callbacks and release recovery. Compare expected counters against local fixtures. Inspect the release artifact to verify that debug route recording remains excluded.
  • Inject a rule-write failure midway through a window on a connected test phone. Measure fault-to-receipt and receipt-to-dashboard delay, verify the fault appears before the window closes, and then verify one failure in the final rate. Repeat offline, across overlapping prayers, past midnight, after a missed alarm and with an overdue release that later clears. Confirm the earlier build and prayer day retain late reports. Test the alert route before increasing rollout exposure.
  • Send test faults and completed windows for two different models from the same manufacturer on the same Android version. Confirm the first fault shows its model, filters keep the two models distinct, and the failure rate uses the matching model’s eligible attempts. Exercise an unknown model and delivery after an OS update.
  • Submit a missing pin near an existing listing, a simple correction, a partial correction on a multi-part mosque, a stale correction and an invalid shape. Follow one accepted example through the queue, catalogue validation, Android import, upgrade of a saved area and receipt update.

Apply the same retention rules to logs and backups. Exclude geometry and answers from request logs.
  • Set a minimum reporting cohort before showing segmented rates. Apply access control for reviewers, transport encryption, size limits and bounded retry on the new endpoint. Check publication terms for user-contributed geometry and explain what can become public.
